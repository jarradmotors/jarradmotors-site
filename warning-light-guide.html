What does my dashboard warning light mean?

Use this guide to understand the common meaning, how urgent it may be and the safest next step. It is general advice for motorists in Casterton and western Victoria—not a substitute for the instructions for your exact vehicle.

Choose the response, not just the colour

Colour is a useful starting point, but the exact symbol, whether it is flashing and how the vehicle feels all matter. Use these five action levels to decide what to do next.

1

Stop safely now

Switch the engine off where advised and do not continue until it is safe.

2

Stop and seek advice

The vehicle may stop or another symptom may make continued driving unsafe.

3

Drive cautiously

Only if the vehicle feels normal, then arrange prompt professional diagnosis.

4

Check or monitor

Inspect the obvious item safely and book help if the warning remains.

5

Routine reminder

Arrange scheduled maintenance; treat any separate warning independently.

◆Do not assume every amber light is safe to drive with, or that every red light has one identical response. A flashing light, a second warning or symptoms such as smoke, overheating, poor braking, heavy steering, shaking or power loss increase the urgency.

1

Stop safely now

These warnings can indicate an immediate risk of major damage or unsafe operation. Pull over somewhere safe as soon as possible.

Oil pressure warning

Urgency: stop now

What it commonly indicates
Engine oil pressure is too low. This is not simply a reminder that an oil change is due.
Safest immediate action
Pull over safely, switch the engine off and do not restart it. After the engine has cooled, follow the owner’s manual if it gives a safe oil-level check procedure.
Who to call
Arrange breakdown assistance. The vehicle may need to be transported for diagnosis rather than driven.

Do not: keep driving, or assume adding oil has fixed a pressure problem.

Engine temperature or overheating

Urgency: stop now

What it commonly indicates
The engine is too hot or the cooling system cannot control its temperature.
Safest immediate action
Pull over safely, switch the engine off and allow it to cool. Arrange help if the warning remains, coolant is leaking or steam is present.
Who to call
Use breakdown assistance when the vehicle has overheated. Book the workshop for cooling-system diagnosis after it is safely recovered.

Do not: remove a radiator or coolant cap while the engine is hot.

Brake system warning

Urgency: stop if it stays on

What it commonly indicates
The parking brake may still be applied, brake fluid may be low or the braking system may have a fault.
Safest immediate action
When safely stationary, check that the parking brake is fully released. If the light remains on, the pedal feels different, braking is reduced or fluid is leaking, do not continue.
Who to call
Arrange breakdown assistance for any braking concern. If the light clears after releasing the parking brake and braking feels normal, monitor it and seek advice if it returns.

Do not: drive to the workshop with reduced braking, a soft pedal or a suspected fluid leak.

Flashing check-engine light

Urgency: stop and assess

What it commonly indicates
A serious engine or emissions fault, often a misfire that can quickly damage the catalytic converter.
Safest immediate action
Ease off the accelerator and pull over safely. Switch off the engine if it is shaking, running roughly, losing power, smoking or showing another serious warning.
Who to call
Seek workshop or breakdown advice before travelling further. Recovery is safer when the engine is running badly.

Do not: continue a journey while the light is flashing and the engine is misfiring.

2

Stop and seek advice

The vehicle may still run briefly, but it can stop without much warning or another symptom may make it unsafe.

Battery or charging-system warning

Urgency: may stop soon

What it commonly indicates
The vehicle’s charging system is not working correctly. The battery symbol does not always mean the battery itself is the cause.
Safest immediate action
Find a safe place to stop and switch off unnecessary electrical loads. If steering becomes heavy, the temperature rises or another red warning appears, stop the engine immediately.
Who to call
Call breakdown assistance if you are on the road. If the vehicle is safely parked, call the workshop to arrange charging-system and battery testing.

Do not: assume a replacement battery alone will fix a charging fault.

3

Drive cautiously and arrange prompt diagnosis

Limited driving may be reasonable only when the vehicle feels completely normal and no more serious warning or message is present.

Steady check-engine light

Urgency: diagnose promptly

What it commonly indicates
The engine, emissions or powertrain control system has detected a fault. A fault code is a starting point, not a complete diagnosis.
Safest immediate action
If the light is steady, the vehicle feels normal and there are no other warnings, drive gently and arrange diagnosis soon. Stop if it begins flashing or symptoms appear.
Who to call
Book the workshop for proper testing. Use the dedicated Engine Light page for more detail without guessing at a cause.

ABS warning

Urgency: drive cautiously

What it commonly indicates
The anti-lock braking system may be unavailable, although normal hydraulic braking may still operate.
Safest immediate action
Allow extra stopping distance, avoid hard braking and arrange diagnosis promptly. If the red brake warning is also on or the pedal feels wrong, stop and call for help.
Who to call
Book the workshop when it is the ABS light alone and braking feels normal. Call breakdown assistance for combined brake warnings or abnormal braking.

Traction or stability control

Urgency: check how it behaves

What it commonly indicates
A flashing light often means the system is actively controlling wheel slip. A light that stays on may mean the system is switched off or has a fault.
Safest immediate action
Reduce speed and avoid harsh acceleration, braking and steering—especially on gravel or wet roads. Check the owner’s manual for your exact symbol.
Who to call
Book diagnosis if the light stays on, will not reset as the manual describes or appears with ABS or brake warnings.

Airbag or SRS warning

Urgency: book promptly

What it commonly indicates
The airbag, seatbelt pretensioner or supplementary restraint system has detected a fault.
Safest immediate action
The vehicle may still drive normally, but one or more restraints may not work correctly in a crash. Arrange diagnosis as soon as practical.
Who to call
Book the workshop for SRS testing and related auto electrical diagnosis.

Do not: unplug or probe airbag wiring or yellow SRS connectors yourself.

DPF or diesel warning

Urgency: follow the exact message

What it commonly indicates
The diesel particulate filter may be loading with soot, or the diesel emissions system has detected a fault.
Safest immediate action
If the warning is steady and the vehicle otherwise feels normal, follow only the regeneration instructions in the owner’s manual. If it is flashing, power is reduced, smoke appears or the check-engine light is also on, seek advice.
Who to call
Book prompt diagnosis when the warning persists. Call breakdown assistance if the vehicle enters reduced-power mode or appears unsafe.

Do not: attempt a generic “DPF burn” drive unless your vehicle’s manual specifically instructs you to do so under the current conditions.

4

Check or monitor

Make a safe basic check, but do not continue if there is visible damage, the vehicle will not start or the warning is joined by a more serious symptom.

Tyre-pressure monitoring

Urgency: inspect safely

What it commonly indicates
One or more tyres may be under-inflated, or the tyre-pressure monitoring system may have a sensor fault.
Safest immediate action
Pull over somewhere safe and inspect the tyres. If they look sound, check cold pressures against the vehicle placard or owner’s manual.
Who to call
Use breakdown assistance for a flat, damaged tyre or unstable handling. Book help if correct pressures do not clear the warning.

Do not: drive on a visibly flat or damaged tyre, or inflate it to the maximum number printed on the tyre sidewall.

Key or immobiliser warning

Urgency: may prevent starting

What it commonly indicates
The vehicle may not recognise the key, the key battery may be weak or the immobiliser system may have a fault.
Safest immediate action
Follow the dash message and owner’s manual. If available, try the correctly programmed spare key. If the engine will not start, treat it as a breakdown.
Who to call
Contact the workshop for diagnosis or see our Automotive Keys service. Use breakdown assistance if you are stranded.

Routine service reminder

A service reminder is usually about scheduled maintenance rather than an immediate fault, but it should not be confused with a separate warning light.

Service reminder or spanner

Urgency: arrange maintenance

What it commonly indicates
Scheduled servicing is due or approaching. Some vehicles also use a spanner symbol for other messages, so check the owner’s manual and dash text.
Safest immediate action
Arrange the required service. Treat any separate red, amber or flashing warning according to its own meaning.
Who to call
Book Jarrad Motors for routine servicing and repairs during normal workshop hours.

Do not: reset the reminder without completing the maintenance that is due.

Before you call or book

If it is safe to do so, having these details ready can make the next step quicker:

  • Vehicle make, model and year
  • The exact symbol, colour and dash message
  • Whether the light is steady or flashing
  • Any noise, smell, smoke, shaking, power loss or change in braking
  • What the vehicle was doing when the warning appeared

A clear photo of the dashboard can help—only take one when the vehicle is safely parked.

Dashboard warning light questions

Short answers to the questions drivers most often need when a light appears.

Can I keep driving with a dashboard warning light on?

It depends on the exact warning and how the vehicle behaves. Stop for oil pressure, overheating, serious brake warnings or a badly running engine with a flashing check-engine light. A steady amber warning may allow a cautious short trip, but only when the vehicle feels normal and the owner’s manual does not tell you to stop.

Why do several warning lights come on when I start the car?

Many lights briefly illuminate as part of the vehicle’s self-check. They should usually go out after the engine starts. A light that stays on, flashes or appears while driving needs attention.

Is a red warning always more serious than an amber one?

Red usually signals greater urgency, but colour alone is not enough. A flashing amber engine light can require you to stop, while some red symbols may simply show that the parking brake is applied. Check the exact symbol, message, symptoms and owner’s manual.

What if the warning light goes out again?

Note what happened and monitor the vehicle. Intermittent faults can leave useful information stored in the vehicle even after the light disappears. Book diagnosis if the light returns, performance changes or the warning relates to a safety system.

Should I clear the warning light with a scan tool?

Clearing a code does not repair the cause and can remove information that helps diagnosis. Record the warning and arrange proper testing instead of repeatedly resetting it.
